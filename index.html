Ask the studio

Type a plain-English what-if or click an example. This box is a rule-based question parser and is not a live AI model. It matches your question against a short list of patterns and runs the real model on it. The answer appears as a preview, and your base case stays unchanged until you press Keep.

It understands what-if changes to growth, terminal growth, margin, WACC, beta, exit multiple or price. It can also find the price that implies a given terminal growth, or solve for growth, margin or WACC at a price. The bear, base and bull cases work too.

Where to get free real data
  • For prices, open the stock on Yahoo Finance and choose Historical Data. Set the range and a Weekly or Monthly frequency, then download the CSV. If the download button is not available to you, copy the table from the page. Do the same for a market index such as the S&P 500 and use the adjusted close for both. Put the two columns side by side in a spreadsheet, matched by date, label them date,stock,market and paste them above. Three years of weekly data gives about 150 returns.
  • For financials, use the company's annual report (Form 10-K, free on sec.gov/edgar). Revenue and operating income (EBIT) come from the income statement. Depreciation and amortization, capital expenditures and the change in working capital come from the cash flow statement. Enter the working capital change so that a build-up is positive. Net debt is total debt minus cash on the balance sheet, and shares outstanding are on the cover page or in the earnings per share note. Five or six years is enough.
  • The app never fetches anything, so you bring the numbers. It works offline with no login or key. For a new quarter or year, paste only the new rows and choose a merge option.

How Claude built and checked this

Plain-English summary

Methodology: every formula
  1. Revenue. Rev_t = Rev_(t-1) x (1 + g_t). Growth fades in a straight line from the year 1 rate to the last-year rate, following g_t = g_1 + (g_N - g_1) x (t-1)/(N-1). Rev_0 is the latest year in the financial history.
  2. EBIT. The margin fades the same way from the year 1 margin to the last-year margin. EBIT_t = Rev_t x margin_t, EBITDA_t = EBIT_t + D&A_t.
  3. Unlevered free cash flow. UFCF_t = EBIT_t x (1 - tax) + D&A_t - Capex_t - NWC% x (Rev_t - Rev_(t-1)), D&A and capex as constant percentages of revenue.
  4. CAPM and WACC. Re = rf + beta x ERP. WACC = (1 - wd) x Re + wd x Rd x (1 - tax), with wd the target debt weight.
  5. Beta. Slope of r_stock = a + b x r_market on simple returns P_t / P_(t-1) - 1. se(b) = sqrt( SSE/(n-2) / Sxx ). 95% interval b +/- t(0.975, n-2) x se(b). Blume adjusted beta 0.67 x b + 0.33. Rolling beta repeats the regression on each window of consecutive returns.
  6. Discounting. PV_t = UFCF_t / (1+WACC)^t, or /(1+WACC)^(t-0.5) with the mid-year convention.
  7. Gordon growth terminal value. TV = UFCF_N x (1+g) / (WACC - g), valid only when g < WACC. Discounted by (1+WACC)^N, or (1+WACC)^(N-0.5) under mid-year, because its cash flows also arrive mid-year.
  8. Exit multiple terminal value. TV = multiple x EBITDA_N, discounted by (1+WACC)^N.
  9. Cross-checks. Implied exit multiple = TV_Gordon x (1+WACC)^(0.5 if mid-year) / EBITDA_N. For the implied perpetual growth from the exit value, let V = TV_exit / (1+WACC)^(0.5 if mid-year). Then g = (V x WACC - UFCF_N) / (V + UFCF_N).
  10. Bridge. EV = sum PV(UFCF) + PV(TV), Equity = EV - net debt, Value per share = Equity / shares, terminal share of EV = PV(TV) / EV.
  11. Reverse DCF. Find x with ValuePerShare(x) = price, every other input at the base case. The app scans the allowed range for a sign change of ValuePerShare(x) - price, then bisects (200 halvings). For the revenue CAGR, growth is held at x in every year and compared with the base path's CAGR (Rev_N/Rev_0)^(1/N) - 1.
  12. Sensitivity and scenarios. The grids recompute the whole DCF for every cell. Scenarios add stated changes to the base case. Weighted value = sum p_i x value_i, probabilities rescaled to 100%.
  13. Tornado. Each input moves up and down by a fixed shock while everything else stays at base. The shocks are growth 2 pts, margin 2 pts, WACC 1 pt, beta 0.2, terminal growth 0.5 pt, exit multiple 2x, capex 1 pt and tax 3 pts. Bars are sorted by the size of the swing in value per share.
  14. Monte Carlo. The seeded generator (mulberry32) gives each draw five standard normals (Box-Muller, cosine branch). They shift revenue growth, the whole margin path, beta, terminal growth and the exit multiple. Terminal growth is capped 0.5 point below that draw's WACC, and the multiple is floored at 1x. The run reports the mean, the standard deviation and the 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 95th percentiles of value per share (linear interpolation) under the headline method.
Methodology: data requirements and assumptions the app makes
  • For prices, we need at least 3 rows to compute any beta and about 30 returns for a usable one. The prices should be adjusted closes with one row per date, and the stock and market must share the same dates. Returns are simple returns with no risk-free adjustment.
  • For financials, we need at least 2 consecutive years to get growth and 4 or more to get useful averages. Every column uses the same units, and the labels assume millions. Capex is positive, and so is an increase in working capital.
  • The model assumes constant D&A, capex and tax percentages, a straight-line fade of growth and margin, and a constant target capital structure. Cash flows arrive evenly through the year (mid-year) or at year end. The exit multiple applies to final-year EBITDA. Net debt comes from the latest balance sheet and stays fixed.
  • It leaves out stock-based compensation, leases, pensions, minority interests, option dilution, currency effects, cyclical swings inside the forecast and changes in capital structure.
Limits to keep in mind
  • A beta from a few years of data has a wide confidence interval and changes with the sample and the return frequency (see rolling beta).
  • Terminal value usually holds most of the value, so small changes in the discount rate or terminal growth move the answer a lot. Use the sensitivity tables, the tornado chart and the Monte Carlo band along with the single number.
  • The reverse DCF changes one assumption at a time. Several different assumption mixes can produce the same price.
  • Validation catches typing errors but cannot catch a wrong source, and the demo numbers are invented. This is an educational tool and not investment advice.
